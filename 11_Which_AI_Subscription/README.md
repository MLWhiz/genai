# Which AI Subscription is Actually Worth Your Money?

Read the post: [Which AI Subscription is Actually Worth Your Money?](https://www.mlwhiz.com/p/which-ai-subscription-is-actually)

Date: 2025-07-03

Subtitle: The Real-World Guide for Developers and Non-Developers alike

## Summary

The author, an ML engineer who has worked at Meta and Roku, compares the AI subscriptions he has tested for months. His TL;DR: if you code, get Claude Pro plus Cursor. For research and admin, start with Gemini's free tier. Skip annual plans because the space moves too fast.

Claude is his "advisor." The Projects feature (long-term context, knowledge base, no re-explaining your stack) is what keeps him there. He built a "Health" project that acts as a gym trainer remembering past weights and reps. He also likes Artifacts for dashboards and rates Claude 4.0 highly for clean code. Cons: no image generation, Projects is Pro-only, and usage limits hit sooner with reasoning on. Pro is listed at $17 in the pricing line (and $20/month earlier in the post), Max at $100 with 5x the usage.

Cursor is his "assistant," an IDE that runs models like Claude 4.0. He vibe coded a site in a weekend with Google auth, Supabase, Stripe, two-factor auth and Vercel deployment. Pro is $20 and Ultra is $200 (20x usage). Gemini is his "researcher," good for tax, business admin and compliance, with a generous free tier. Google AI Pro is $19 and includes 2 TB storage.

ChatGPT Plus ($20/month) is the generalist (Canvas, voice, DALL-E, Sora, GPT Store), but he doesn't pay for it. The post also lists Copilot, Cline, Replit, Jasper, Midjourney, Firefly, Synthesia, Descript, Runway, Notion AI and Julius AI. On economics, he cites a 55% faster task completion figure and argues that a 50% boost on four hours a month at $100/hour pays for most plans.

## Key takeaways

- Developers: start with Claude Pro, then add Cursor once copy-pasting code becomes the bottleneck.
- Researchers and business users: use Gemini's free tier until you hit limits.
- Claude Projects is the feature the author says he'd miss most.
- Choose monthly plans over annual ones because tools change fast.
- Start with one subscription that fits your biggest pain point.
